## Starting from First Principles

Since its inception, marketing has often been confused with disciplines in sales or advertising, while its role within economics and management remains largely misunderstood.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Jsqo!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2c554a66-b674-45de-87b3-79a4d21527d4_3600x1200.jpeg)

When I first started deciding which marketing (and branding) topics were worth writing about, I ran into a recurring problem: most people believe they understand marketing — but very few understand its fundamentals.

So the only honest way to explain marketing is to start from the beginning. In order to write about more advanced topics, we need to create a strong foundation first.

This essay does not follow the conventional explanations commonly found online. Instead, its purpose is to reconstruct the concept of marketing from two underlying problems that precede it:

- scarcity, studied by economics;
- and the organization of resources, studied by management.

> *“…a people without understanding will come to ruin!” **\-*** **Hosea 4: 14b**

You may already understand what marketing is and how it works. If so, you are part of a minority. Consider the evidence:

- In a 2018 study, only a third of businesses questioned knew the concept of marketing
- A 2025 report from The CMO Survey found that 64% of CMOs identify proving marketing’s financial impact as their primary challenge.

In my observation, most people fall into one of three categories:

- They confuse marketing with sales or advertising;
- They have a superficial understanding and cannot connect marketing to broader management systems;
- Or they do not understand it at all.

This essay addresses the first two. We will:

- Define marketing’s foundational disciplines: economics and management;
- Examine how they relate to one another;
- And arrive at a structural definition of marketing.

(Branding will be addressed in Part II.)

## Economics Explains Choice Under Scarcity

We begin with economics.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!PBR9!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ff5f358ab-fae2-4750-b73d-7168ec6a8a93_1184x864.png)

James Otteson argues that economics was formally established as a discipline in the 18th century under the name political economy. Early economists such as Adam Smith and David Hume sought to apply Newtonian scientific methodology to the study of human behavior and society.

**Their objectives were twofold:**

- to identify behavioral patterns through observation and historical analysis;
- and to use those patterns to inform institutional design and reform.

## However, economic behavior existed long before economics became a formal discipline.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!faUR!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe949a16c-1643-4c04-8f5f-07ca2a44e8b8_3600x1200.jpeg)

References to economic reasoning can be found in:

- Ancient Greece, in the works of Aristotle, Plato, and Xenophon;
- There was no significant progress in economics in ancient Rome (although it was important for administration);
- Later developments such as *mercantilism* in the 16th century;
- And *physiocracy* in 18th-century France attempted to explain wealth, production, and exchange.

## After the 18th century

The two objectives of Political Economy were divided into two schools of thought:

- **Moral Philosophy**: which is a vision of *how things should be* - what our objectives should be from a moral point of view. Also, what the norms should be for these objectives.
- **Economics**: being a technical and descriptive analysis of human behavior in specific contexts (nowadays, economists also use mathematical models to aid in their analyses.)

Economics emerged as the systematic study of a problem that had always existed: Scarcity. As defined in modern economic theory:

> *“Economics is the study of how individuals, institutions, and society choose to deal with the condition of scarcity \[...\], From an isolated individual to the largest society on the planet, everyone is constantly involved in the struggle to survive, balance the budget, and even prosper, given the relative scarcity they face”*

This is the fundamental constraint of all human decision-making. Resources are limited. Human wants are not. The primary categories of scarce resources include:

- **Land:** natural resources such as minerals, water, and physical space.
- **Labor:** human effort, skill, and expertise.
- **Capital:** tools, infrastructure, and productive assets

## Trade-offs and Opportunity Costs: The Rationale For the Existence of Marketing

Because resources are limited, every decision implies a trade-off. Choosing one option means rejecting another.

This introduces one of the central concepts of economics: **opportunity cost**, the value of the alternative that was not chosen. For example:

- If a consumer chooses a donut instead of a hamburger, the rejected hamburger represents an opportunity cost.

This is the environment in which marketing becomes relevant: **Marketing exists within the structure of trade-offs**. Its role is not to eliminate scarcity, but to influence choice within it.

> *”People don’t know what they want until you show it to them”,* **Steve Jobs**

In short, economics studies: how do agents use their resources? What are the consequences of their choices?

## Management: The Organization of Scarce Resources

Nelson Pereira da Costa defines Management as:

> *”A set of isolated concepts, principles, rules, methods, functions, and techniques, the knowledge of which assists the manager in achieving the organization’s objectives, using its own or acquired resources. The word administration comes from Latin, meaning ad (direction towards, tendency towards), and minister (subordination or obedience) and means the one who performs a function under someone’s command, that is, the one who provides a service to another. Administration means subordination and service.”*

In essence, the main difference between economics and management is:

- **economics** studies how scarce resources are allocated broadly,
- **management** studies how organizations allocate resources internally to achieve specific objectives.

Management addresses a practical problem: Given limited resources, how can an organization achieve its goals efficiently and effectively? And it involves, in order to produce the desired outcomes:

- Planning;
- Organizing;
- Directing;
- And Controlling Resources.

Because of scarcity constraint, every organization must decide:

- Where to invest
- What to prioritize
- How to maximize returns on limited resources

And this naturally leads to a critical organization function: the function for generating demand. This function is Marketing

## Marketing Exists to Influence Resource Allocation

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Js4t!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F579d0406-6501-4c65-a46a-1c5cd6cce49c_1228x768.jpeg)

Philip Kotler, the world's foremost authority on marketing

We already found the meaning of our two foundational disciplines. Now we can reach Marketing.

> *“The business of businesse is business“,* **Milton Friedman, Nobel laureate in Economics**

Most organizations exist to sustain themselves economically. To do so, they must generate revenue. To generate revenue, they must create demand.

**Marketing operates at the intersection of economics and management:**

- From an **economic** perspective, marketing influences how people allocate their scarce resources - attention, money and time
- From a **management** perspective, marketing is a strategic function that helps organizations achieve growth objectives by acquiring, retaining and expanding customer relationships

## Marketing is a Market Act

A Market exists wherever three elements interact:

1. Supply (organizations selling something)
2. Demand (people willing to buy something)
3. The mechanisms and rules that connect them

Marketing governs that connection:

- It reduces friction between supply and demand,
- It aligns value creation with value perception,
- It influences how choices are made under conditions of scarcity

## Structural Definition of Marketing

Based on this framework, marketing can be defined as:

> **The organizational function responsible for influencing how and where individuals and institutions allocate their scarce resources to a company's products or services—not just once, but repeatedly over time. To achieve this, it seeks out the customers most compatible with the organization.**

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!1mPs!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Faf9cc7cb-f64e-4ade-b473-61172cb27e52_2560x1440.jpeg)

Marketing does not begin with campaigns, channels, or tactics.

It begins with **scarcity** — the condition that makes choice unavoidable.

**Economics** explains *why* choices exist.  
**Management** explains *how* resources are allocated in response to those choices.

**Marketing** exists to influence *which options absorb those scarce resources* — attention, time, money, trust.

Until marketing is understood through this lens, it will remain confused with execution, rather than recognized as a core discipline of management.

**And Branding explains why some choices stop feeling like choices at all — as we will explore next.**
